I'll admit it: I'm a big fan of the second Tron movie, and I actually like it better than the first one. I know: blasphemy. After all, the second one is generally considered
not that great a film. But I'm somehow too young for the first Tron, or rather I just didn't grow up with it, and audiovisually I think the second one is an absolute banger!
I'm not really a big Daft Punk fan, but combined with the visuals from this movie it simply brings me a whole lot of joy!

But I'm not a film critic, I'm a pinball tinkerer, modder, freak, hehe! So this isn't about the movie, it's about the Stern Pinball machine from 2011.
That was the phase when Stern was the very last pinball manufacturer left standing and built fairly stripped-down machines. Why stripped-down? Well, in the 2010s
you certainly weren't getting rich with pinball machines. Williams had shut down its pinball business about ten years earlier and the market was on the floor, so everything had to be built on a budget,
and you can tell when you look at the machines from that era (in my opinion).

Still, Tron: Legacy turned out to be what we'd now call a modern classic. A machine with great atmosphere, great integration of the theme, and a whole lot of
fun to play (opinions famously differ on that one, but I think it's great! Not least because of the sound :D).

There was a Special Edition limited to 400 units that had a few cool gimmicks. Among other things, illuminated ramps using a kind of light tube. In the game, the ramps basically represent
those funky light cycles they ride around on in the movie. You know, the ones that trail those light beams behind them, and if you drive into one
you're pretty much "Game Over". I've always thought that was implemented really nicely, and in general that 80s aesthetic and all the arcade stuff just gets me anyway.

Unfortunately, we only have a Pro version of the machine at the pinball museum, and it doesn't have those SE gimmicks. But since you can always count on the pinball community and
somebody is always making something they can sell, there's a cool substitute that actually makes the whole thing even better than on the SE version.

## The Idea(s)

When you're browsing the internet and you've just gotten a new machine into the museum that you actually think is pretty awesome, you usually start poking
around to see what great mods you could install (especially if you only managed to grab the Pro edition). At least for me that's completely normal :D

There's a long-running project in the pinball scene called "Pinduino" that lets you spruce up boring machines with great additional lighting effects in the form of
addressable LED strips (<a href="https://github.com/elyons/pinduino" target="_blank">Pinduino project on GitHub</a>).

The basic principle is that you plug an add-on board with an Arduino onto the pinball boards and tap, for example, the control signals of coils, flashers or optos,
and then use software on the Arduino to drive additional hardware (in this case addressable LED strips) in response to those "events".

On Pinside I then fairly quickly stumbled across this: <a href="https://pinside.com/pinball/shops/shop/1025-professor-pinball/08572-tron-interactive-ramp-lighting-20-gel-cob-leds" target="_blank">Tron Interactive Ramp Lighting 2.0 (Gel COB LEDs)</a>

## Implementation

For the Pinduino, I had a look on Flippermarkt.de and found someone who sells ready-made Pinduino boards. As luck would have it, he still had one for Stern Whitestar left over.
Then I quickly bought LED strips like the ones in the video on AliExpress. These are so-called COB LED strips.

The abbreviation COB stands for "chip-on-board" and describes an LED technology in which several COB LED chips are placed directly on an aluminium board.
A COB LED is a kind of little lamp that can shine very brightly. Imagine that instead of having one single small light bulb, you put lots of tiny lights
very close together on a small plate. Because these many lights are so close to each other, it looks as if it were just one big light. Compared to LED strips
with SMD LEDs, COB LED strips produce a homogeneous light without visible light dots.

Since I had recently installed a colour display based on PPUC and ZeDMD in the Tron, which also taps 12V from the machine, I wanted to add proper power distribution
for the mods, so I ordered a distribution board on eBay that splits the 12V across four fuses. Better safe than sorry.

Kindly enough, the forum member had already flashed the software for my project onto the Pinduino, so I didn't really have to do anything there. It was
plug & play, so to speak ;)

If you need the files otherwise, you'll find the program code for all kinds of projects (Pinduino supports other machines too) on GitHub. All I basically had to do was use the script
and the author's instructions to figure out what length I needed to cut the LED strips to.

### So, on to the topper. That one had to be built first...

The whole construction basically works like this: you print the letters as hollow bodies, install LED lighting, then slap a diffuser made of transparent material on
top, followed by the "line patterns" over that, so it looks like neon tubes.

So first, everything got printed in several runs:

<LightboxImage
    src="/images/hobby/tron_modding/tron_sign_print.png"

Then came the question of what lighting to put in there. From an older project I still had some cheapo RGB LED strip from the
hardware store, the kind you control with a crappy little RF remote. I simply cut it into several pieces and connected them at the back using splitters I also still had lying around from the Alien pinball.

Then I glued everything together with superglue, and just like that the thing was ready and looked really damn sexy!
